## 3. The ML layer: how it can add value now

The first model tried to say "up or down" for every stock-day, and it could not (AUC ≈ 0.50). The idea worth building is narrower: **an ML layer on top of the rules, scoring the setups we already take.**

1. **M2: setup quality score (meta-labeling).**
   - **What it predicts:** for each scanner / Scout setup, P(target before stop within 5 sessions).
   - **Training data:** the same detectors replayed over 2020–2026 on the copied history, so every historical setup becomes a labelled example (thousands, instead of the ~140 live alerts so far).
   - **Features:**
     - the setup itself: detector, R:R, ATR %, distance to support/resistance;
     - the stock: RS rank, MA trend, 52-week distance, volume ratio, sector strength;
     - the market: regime, VIX, breadth;
     - events: results distance, news count;
     - the Time Machine's own numbers.
   - **Method:** LightGBM + isotonic calibration, purged and embargoed walk-forward.
   - **Use:**
     - ranks the day's candidates;
     - adds a gate (P ≥ threshold);
     - sizes within the frozen caps (higher P → up to the 1 % risk, never above).
2. **Calibrated odds.** M2's probability next to the Time Machine's, both checked against real outcomes (a calibration plot); after ~100 graded trades this shows which one to trust.
3. **Learning loop (the "reward/penalty" goal).**
   - Every graded outcome feeds a weekly **challenger** retrain.
   - The live **champion** stays frozen.
   - A challenger replaces it only after it beats the champion in shadow on forward data (a fixed rule, written down before).
4. **Regime model.** A small model for "trend vs range" days on the index (breadth, VIX, ADX), to switch the setups on or off: breakouts on trend days, support reversals on range days.
5. **Intraday (later).** The same meta-labeling on 15m setups once the 15m odds engine and the recorder's history (~60 sessions) exist.

**How we will know it works:** the paper portfolio run three ways: pf-v1 rules alone, rules + M2 score, and the Nifty 500 benchmark, all from the same start. M2 counts as useful only if "rules + M2" beats "rules alone" net of costs over the forward window. No change to pf-v1 mid-test: M2 runs as a parallel book.
